**Form-check apps (evidence):**
- Phone squat detection ranged from 0% (side view at 90 cm) to 95.5% (diagonal at 45 degrees and 200 cm). Camera placement mattered more than the app (JMIR mHealth 2026, via https://kensoforge.com/insights/ai-form-checker-computer-vision-lifting-apps-2026).
- A 2025 systematic review says camera-based screening apps' accuracy "remains to be thoroughly evaluated".
- Vendor accuracy claims (such as 95%) are not independently published.
- Main source: https://www.sensai.fit/blog/best-ai-workout-form-check-apps-2026. The Alibaba-hosted articles are unverifiable, so I discounted them.

**What a hobby project can and cannot legitimately derive (modelling honesty):**
- **Static optimisation** is the standard OpenSim way to get activation estimates. It assumes rigid tendons. It is "inherently biased against the prediction of antagonistic muscle activation" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350453322000406).
- **Squat-specific validation** exists but is thin. In elite powerlifters, activations agreed with surface EMG "for all analysed muscles except" rectus femoris, which was "virtually absent in the simulation despite clear EMG activity" (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/peerReview?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0327973).
- **Static optimisation validity** is task-, muscle- and phase-dependent (https://doi.org/10.3390/biomechanics3040044). Surface EMG also has cross-talk.
- **Agreement with EMG is qualitative (pattern-level), not quantitative, in what I found.** This is consistent with VISION.md's "band, never a number" rule. It is evidence for that rule, not just a stylistic choice.
- **Who blurs the line:** I could not establish whether any product explicitly labels its activation as illustrative or modelled. Muscle & Motion has no methodology page that I could find. Fitbod and Boostcamp document their inputs but not their validation. **I did not find a product that is carefully labelled "modelled" in the market, nor one confirmed as blurring it.** Uncertain.

- **ACWR evidence (relevant to the honest load model):**
  - A 2026 multilevel meta-analysis (16 studies, 797 athletes) found a small-to-moderate association with injury (g = 0.35, 95% CI 0.16–0.54, I² 95.8%). It concluded the evidence does not support ACWR as a stand-alone causal or predictive model (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13520966/).

### 7. Audience evidence

- **Coach adoption barriers:**
  - Lack of time, expertise and resources, and a risk of over-reliance on data (scoping review: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17479541241247131).
  - Czech football survey of 235 coaches: lack of resources was the top barrier (74.5%), and 75% of S&C coaches named human resources (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11825800/).
  - US collegiate coaches cited cost and sport-specific adaptability (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12434470/).
  - 113 US S&C coaches and athletic trainers said inconsistent measurements undermine trust (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337026572_State-of-the-art_review_of_athletic_wearable_technology_What_113_strength_and_conditioning_coaches_and_athletic_trainers_from_the_USA_said_about_technology_in_sports). The evidence is football- and collegiate-heavy, so it is only indirectly about S&C.
- **Athlete abandonment:**
  - A 2022 survey of 209 mHealth users found amotivation, loss of interest, and trying other apps were the drivers (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8872344/).
  - A scoping review found sharp early abandonment that then slows (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11694054/).
  - Blog stats (about 70% gone within 100 days; about 80% within a month) are unverified.
  - Manual data entry is cited as a top reason in one national survey, via a vendor blog (https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-churn-retention/).
- **Does interactive 3D anatomy improve learning?**
  - A 2025 systematic review of 27 RCTs found virtual technologies were comparable or superior to traditional methods. The largest gains were in complex spatial topics, and low spatial-ability learners gained most (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12061000/).
  - A 2024 meta-analysis found moderate gains in test scores, satisfaction and enjoyment, but no time saving (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38671399).
  - Stereoscopic viewing with non-interactive images showed no benefit (https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/medu.14352).
  - One trial found interactive 3D neither helped nor hurt versus an informationally equivalent textbook, though students liked it more.
  - **All of this is medical-student anatomy education. I found no studies of 3D muscle-activation visualisation for lifters or S&C coaching outcomes. That is a real evidence gap, so no outcome claim can be made.**

### 8. Constraints and obligations

- **Australian Privacy Act:**
  - OAIC guidance (B.81 / s 6D(4)(b)) says the small business exemption does not cover an entity that provides a health service and holds health information. Fitness professionals who record health details are listed as examples by a secondary source (https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts · https://www.zipitsolutions.com.au/insights/privacy-act-health-practices-compliance).
  - The statutory privacy tort reportedly commenced 10 June 2025 (secondary source).
  - Removal of the small business exemption is **contested**. One source says 10 December 2026 and others say it is unlegislated. I found no primary government source. **Uncertain.**
  - Whether a free hobby app that stores RHR, HRV, BP and injury notes "provides a health service" is a legal question I did not answer. Get proper advice if real users arrive.
- **App stores:**
  - Apple Guideline 1.4.1 gives extra scrutiny to health or medical apps that could give inaccurate data. Developers must disclose data and methodology, and apps with health information should link to sources (developer forum quotes: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/134169 · https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708478). Check the current guideline text directly.
  - **Google Play health-app policy: not found.**
  - The Capacitor APK path is mentioned in CLAUDE.md. A "modelled, sourced, band not number" approach lines up with the Apple disclosure expectation, though that is my inference.
- **CC BY-SA share-alike:**
  - Consistent with VISION.md: attribution, a statement of changes, and the same licence on the derived data files.
  - **The Z-Anatomy NC components are a trap** if anyone ever swaps in Z-Anatomy parts.
  - I did not independently check how CC BY-SA 2.1 JP treats derived datasets.
- **Accessibility of canvas content:**
  - Canvas content is invisible to the accessibility tree except for fallback content or ARIA. Fallback content must cover the whole canvas and give keyboard access to interactive parts (https://pauljadam.com/demos/canvas.html).
  - A 2025 W3C thread says canvas fallback is "not really fit for purpose" for most cases (https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webapps-github/2025Jun/0274.html).
  - The Chart.js pattern is `role="img"` plus `aria-label`, with the data table as the text alternative (https://www.chartjs.org/docs/latest/general/accessibility.html).
  - I found no WCAG 2.2 normative text on canvas or `aria-hidden`. **Uncertain.**
  - The repo's `aria-hidden` canvas plus a DOM muscle list pattern is consistent with the "text alternative as the data table" approach. This is my inference and not a WCAG ruling.

## Uncovered ground (stated as evidence, not opinion)

1. **No product found that shows modelled muscle activation through the full range of motion, with provenance, in a free web tool.**
   - Closest is Muscle & Motion (animated, $89/yr, no methodology page found). After that come Fitbod and Boostcamp (per-muscle estimates that document inputs but not validation; Fitbod's formula is unpublished), and Hevy (set counts, heatmap method unpublished).
   - MuscleWiki is an exercise browser, not an activation model. The two small 3D web tools (Workout Anatomy, VisualBody Lab) disclose no sourcing in what I saw.
2. **No product found that labels activation as modelled and uses a band instead of a number.** The evidence says pattern-level agreement is all the modelling supports, so this is a defensible gap.
3. **Free anatomy tools exist only as general atlases** (BioDigital Personal, Anatomy Learning base, Z-Anatomy). I found no free atlas keyed to lifts.
4. **No rowing stroke anatomy or sequencing in 3D was found.** Existing rowing tools do logging and force curves (ErgData, ErgZone, RP3, BioRow). Coaches' pain is data admin and team views, not understanding the stroke.
5. **The anatomy-learning evidence base is medical-student only.** Nobody has demonstrated a lifter or coach benefit, so a first-party measure could be a differentiator or an honest gap to label.
6. **Load dashboards are saturated at the free end** (Intervals.icu). No gap there.
7. **Opportunity created by consolidation:** Garmin owns TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic (July 2026). Independence anxiety is explicit in coverage (the Tredict piece). I found no direct evidence of coach migration.

## Recommendation (for the Product Manager to weigh, not adopt blindly)

1. The strongest evidence-backed gap is a free, public, **provenance-labelled** modelled-activation view across the full range of motion. Keep "band, never a number" and the modelled label. The EMG-validation literature supports it.
2. Do not build a catalogue. The incumbent's catalogue (1,200+ exercises) is exactly what a hobby cannot match. Keep the flagship-movement plan from VISION.md.
3. Treat the OpenSim/SimTK model terms review as a blocker before wider distribution. Also confirm OpenCap's terms before any dependency on it. Avoid SMPL model data for anything public.
4. Do not compete on load dashboards. Intervals.icu is free and complete, and TrainingPeaks is now Garmin-owned. Keep the load model but consider ACWR messaging carefully, given the 2026 meta-analysis says it is not a stand-alone predictor.
5. Rowing: no 3D stroke anatomy was found, and coaches' pain is team data admin. If rowing anatomy is pursued, it is a genuine gap, but it needs a new data pipeline. Do not drift into team logging.
6. Add a small honest "how this was modelled and what it cannot say" page. It matches Apple 1.4.1 disclosure expectations (if a store release is ever pursued) and the education literature's engagement findings. Do not claim learning gains, because none are evidenced for lifters.
7. Verify with a lawyer or the OAIC before holding health data (BP, medication, injury) for real users. The exemption position is contested and health-service providers are covered regardless of size.
8. Keep the text-alternative approach for the canvas. Consider a full-canvas `role="img"` with a data-table alternative as the documented pattern. Do not rely on canvas fallback content.
9. Browser 3D: keep a WebGL path. WebGPU support is broad but not primary-source confirmed, and the three.js WebGPURenderer status is unverified.
10. Re-run the blocked primary-page checks (Muscle & Motion methodology, SMPL licence, TrainingPeaks pricing, Workout Anatomy, VisualBody Lab) once those hosts are allowed.
